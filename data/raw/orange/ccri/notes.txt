MARKET AND EXPORT EVIDENCE

The QRT report provides historical evidence on the Indian
citrus market and export potential.

During 2014-15 to 2016-17:
- India exported 17,231.45, 34,524.55 and 47,539.39
  metric tonnes of oranges respectively.
- Total citrus exports during the three-year period were
  1,92,564.94 metric tonnes.
- Mandarin/tangerine exports were 487.05, 2,526.98 and
  1,076.84 metric tonnes respectively.
- Total citrus export value was Rs. 109.11 crore,
  Rs. 169.38 crore and Rs. 223.37 crore respectively.

The report notes that Nagpur Santra (mandarin) exported
from Vidarbha was also included under the orange category
in trade statistics.

The report identifies potential export relevance for
Nagpur mandarin because its availability during April-May
was noted as a possible opportunity for European markets.

Important:
These figures are historical market evidence from the
QRT report and should not be treated as current 2026
market size or current export demand.

Use:
Market/export evidence for future evaluation of citrus
export-related business opportunities.

Source:
QRT Report of ICAR-CCRI (2013-18)

INDUSTRY BACKGROUND EVIDENCE

The QRT report states that India's citrus cultivation area
increased from 90,700 ha in 1961 to 10.77 lakh ha in
2017-18, while production increased from 8.23 lakh tonnes
to 125.10 lakh tonnes.

The report states that average citrus productivity at the
national level was 11.50 tonnes/ha in 2017-18 and that
19-25 tonnes/ha was not uncommon in some Vidarbha orchards.

Use:
Historical industry-scale and regional productivity
context.

ADDITIONAL PROCESSING AND VALUE-ADDITION EVIDENCE

The QRT report states that ICAR-CCRI developed RTS products,
juice powders, carbonated beverages and other nutraceuticals
from different citrus species. These technologies were in
different stages of refinement, with many having been
commercialized.

The report also recommends development of high-value products
such as pectin, flavonoids, oils and antioxidants from citrus
peel and pomace to utilize processing waste and create
additional value.

The report records historical imports of citrus by-products
such as pectins, essential oils and peels:
- Rs. 212.08 crore in 2014-15
- Rs. 324.80 crore in 2017-18

Important:
These import figures are historical evidence and should not
be treated as current 2026 market size or expected revenue.

The QRT also recommends that products and by-products
developed by the Institute should be commercialized,
especially through small-scale units.

This evidence strengthens:
- RTS and Carbonated Citrus Beverages
- Nagpur Mandarin Spray-Dried Powder
- Citrus By-product Utilization

ADDITIONAL AGRICULTURAL SERVICE AND TECHNOLOGY EVIDENCE

The QRT report documents several technologies relevant to
entrepreneurial service models in citrus production:

1. Micro-irrigation and fertigation schedules for
   'Nagpur' mandarin and acid lime.

2. Crop regulation and fruit-drop management.

3. Rejuvenation technology for declining citrus orchards.

4. Raised-bed planting for reducing waterlogging and
   Phytophthora infestation.

5. Pest and disease management technologies.

6. Mechanical pruning, training and spraying technology
   demonstrated in Nagpur and Amravati districts.

Potential Business Candidate:
Mechanized Citrus Orchard Services

Description:
Provision of equipment-based orchard services such as
mechanical pruning, training and spraying for citrus growers.

Evidence:
ICAR-CCRI demonstrated mechanical tree pruning and
electrostatic orchard spraying in Nagpur and Amravati
districts. The report states that mechanical training and
pruning of mature Nagpur mandarin plants resulted in
uniform fruits and enhanced orchard life.

Status:
Candidate - evidence-backed technology; business model
requires further validation.

greater emphasis on micro-irrigation/fertigation redesign

AGRICULTURE EXTENSION, CONTRACT FARMING AND MARKET-LINKAGE EVIDENCE

The QRT report studied contract farming among citrus growers.

For acid lime farming, inadequate irrigation water and price
uncertainty were perceived as major risks. Guaranteed and fixed
pricing structures were perceived as an advantage of contract
farming. Pricing arrangements and payment procedures were
important factors for growers when considering agreements.

The QRT also identified feasibility study of contract farming
in 'Nagpur' mandarin as an area for future work.

Potential Candidate:
Citrus Contract-Farming / Farmer-Buyer Linkage

Status:
Candidate - requires validation of actual buyer networks,
contract structure, legal requirements, aggregation model and
business economics.

The QRT discussions also identified:
- role of FPOs
- market interventions
- GI marketing of 'Nagpur' mandarin
- small-size graders
- packaging requirements
- processing units
- technology-transfer training

These findings should strengthen the future Location, Market
and Infrastructure modules of EntreVision.

Nursery Evidence:
The report studied constraints of citrus nursery owners in
Vidarbha and identified issues including containerized nursery
systems, guaranteed/fixed price structures, minimum support
price, appropriate technology, nursery permits and availability
of rootstocks.

This strengthens:
- Disease-Free Citrus Nursery
- Citrus Rootstock Seed Supply

Extension Evidence:
The report states that production and productivity of a large
number of citrus orchards increased due to trainings and
demonstrations received by citrus growers from ICAR-CCRI,
Nagpur.

Potential Candidate:
Citrus Advisory / Training Services

Status:
Candidate - requires validation of business model, customers,
pricing and service requirements.
