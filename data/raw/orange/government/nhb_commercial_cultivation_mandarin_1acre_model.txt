================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================
Mandarin orange (Citrus reticulata) is most common among citrus fruits grown in India. It occupies nearly 40% of the total area under citrus cultivation in India. The most important commercial citrus species in India are mandarin (Citrus reticulata) 41%, sweet orange (Citrus sinensis) 23%, and acid lime (Citrus aurantifolia) 23%.

================================================================================
2. OBJECTIVE
================================================================================
To present a bankable one-acre model for high quality commercial cultivation of Mandarin Orange.

================================================================================
3. AREA, PRODUCTION & PRODUCTIVITY
================================================================================
- Maharashtra leads orange production with 124.2 thousand hectares area, 1117.5 thousand MT production, and 9.0 MT/ha productivity.
- Important citrus belts in Maharashtra: Nagpur, Akola, Amravati, Wardha (Satpura hills of Vidarbha region).

================================================================================
4. MARKET ANALYSIS & STRATEGY
================================================================================
- India ranks 9th among top orange producing countries contributing 3% to world production.
- Only 1.72% of India's orange production is currently exported.
- Nagpur mandarin is renowned for high quality.
- Mrig crop (monsoon blossom maturing in Feb-March) has great export potential as international arrivals of mandarin fruit in global markets are lowest during this period.
- Major export destinations: Bangladesh (28,229 MT), Nepal, Oman, Sri Lanka, Canada, USA, UK, UAE.
- Distant exports to Europe, Gulf, and SE Asia require refrigerated container ships and pre-cooling/post-harvest treatments.

================================================================================
5. PRODUCTION TECHNOLOGY
================================================================================
5.1 Agro-climatic Requirements:
- Frost-free tropical/subtropical up to 1,500m MSL.
- Rainfall: 100-120 cm.
- Temperature: 10°C to 35°C.
- Soil: Medium or light loamy soils with slightly heavy sub-soil, well-drained, pH 6.0-8.0.

5.2 Spacing & Planting:
- Spacing: 6m x 6m (Square system) accommodating 120 plants/acre.
- Pit size: 50cm x 50cm x 50cm.
- Propagation: T-budding / Shield budding in Jan-Feb; transplanting in July-August.

5.3 Drip Irrigation & Water Requirements:
- Young trees (1-4 years): 5 - 15 Litres/day/tree
- Middle-aged trees (5-8 years): 35 - 105 Litres/day/tree
- Mature trees (9+ years): 60 - 170 Litres/day/tree
- Annual requirement: 900 - 1100 mm/year
- 50-60% water saving with drip irrigation vs flood irrigation.

5.4 Crop Regulation (Bahar Treatment):
- Ambe Bahar: Feb flowering, harvest Nov-Jan
- Mrig Bahar: June flowering, harvest Feb-April (preferred in Central India due to water conservation in April-May resting and higher export window prices).
- Hast Bahar: Oct flowering.

5.5 Intercropping:
- Intercrops during gestation (Years 1-4): Pea, cowpea, gram generating ~Rs. 30,000/year/acre.

5.6 Yield & Lifespan:
- Bearing starts year 4; substantial yield from year 6.
- Yield: 40 quintals (Year 5/6) increasing to 50-80 quintals in mature years (500-800 fruits/tree).
- Average yield: 4.8 tonnes/acre (12 tonnes/ha).
- Productive lifespan: 15-20 years after 5 gestation years.

================================================================================
6. POST-HARVEST MANAGEMENT
================================================================================
- Washing with chlorine (1000 ppm).
- Stayfresh high shine wax (2.5%) with Bavistin (4000 ppm) + tunnel drying at 50-55°C.
- Storage in evaporative cool chambers at 8-10°C & 90-95% RH for up to 3 weeks.
- Degreening with Ethrel (50 ppm) within 5 days.
- Packaging in ventilated corrugated fibreboard (CFB) cartons.
